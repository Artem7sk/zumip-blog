Podcast Show Notes Template: How to Write Notes That Rank, Convert, and Save You Hours

If you publish a podcast, you already know the drill: you finish recording, edit the audio, upload it… and then stare at a blank document trying to write show notes. Again. For most solo podcasters and small teams, this is the part that quietly eats 1–3 hours per episode — time you could spend recording, promoting, or actually resting.

A good podcast show notes template fixes this. Not because templates are magic, but because they remove decisions. When the structure is already there, you're just filling in blanks instead of reinventing your process every week.

This guide breaks down what actually belongs in show notes, gives you a reusable structure, and shows you where most podcasters leave money and traffic on the table.

---

What Show Notes Are Actually For (Three Different Jobs)

Most people treat show notes as one thing. They're really three:

1. For listeners — quick context, links to what you mentioned, timestamps so they can jump to the good part.

2. For search engines — text that helps Google understand what the episode is about and rank it for relevant queries.

3. For you — a source of repurposed content: newsletter blurbs, social posts, quote graphics, YouTube descriptions.

If your current show notes only do job #1, you're doing extra work for less return. A solid template covers all three in one pass.

---

The Core Podcast Show Notes Template (Copy This Structure)

Here's a fill-in-the-blank skeleton you can use for almost any episode format — interview, solo, or co-hosted:

1. Episode Title (SEO-aware, not keyword-stuffed)

    • Format: `[Core topic] with [Guest Name] — [Specific angle or outcome]`
    • Example: `Email Marketing That Doesn't Suck with Jane Doe — Building a List From Zero`

Avoid vague titles like "Episode 47: Great Chat With Jane." Nobody searches for that.

2. One-Sentence Episode Summary

    • 20–40 words.
    • Answer: Who is this for, and what will they get?
    • Example: `Jane shares the exact 3-email sequence she used to grow a 10,000-person list without paid ads.`

3. Key Takeaways (3–6 Bullets)

This is the single most valuable section for both readers and SEO. Each bullet should be a complete, specific idea — not a teaser.

    • ❌ "We talk about email marketing."
    • ✅ "Why a welcome sequence of 3 emails outperforms a single welcome email."

4. Timestamps / Chapter Markers

    • `00:00 — Intro`
    • `02:15 — Why most welcome emails fail`
    • `14:40 — The 3-email sequence, step by step`
    • `31:10 — Tools and metrics Jane tracks`

Timestamps improve listener experience and can earn you a "Key Moments" style appearance in some platforms. Keep them accurate.

5. Resources & Links Mentioned

    • Guest website, social handles
    • Books, tools, articles referenced
    • Your own related episodes or products

6. Guest Bio (if applicable)

    • 2–3 sentences. Keep it factual.

7. A Clear CTA

    • One action: subscribe, leave a review, grab a freebie, or check out a product.

8. Optional: Full Transcript or Key Quotes

    • Transcripts help accessibility and SEO. If you don't want to publish the full thing, pull 2–3 standout quotes.

A Filled-In Example (So You Can See It in Action)

Title: Email Marketing That Doesn't Suck with Jane Doe — Building a List From Zero

Summary: Jane shares the exact 3-email welcome sequence she used to grow a 10,000-person list without paid ads.

Key Takeaways:

    • Why a welcome sequence of 3 emails beats a single welcome email
    • The one metric Jane checks weekly (and the three she ignores)
    • How to write a subject line in under 60 seconds
    • The tool stack she uses for under $30/month

Timestamps:

    • 00:00 — Intro
    • 02:15 — Why most welcome emails fail
    • 14:40 — The 3-email sequence, step by step
    • 31:10 — Tools and metrics

Links: janedoe.com, @janedoe, Email That Converts (book)

CTA: Want the sequence as a swipe file? Grab it here.

That's it. That's a complete, useful set of show notes — and it took maybe 15 minutes once you have the structure.

Where Most Podcasters Lose Time (and How to Fix It)

Problem 1: Starting from a blank page every episode

Fix: Build one master template in Google Docs or Notion. Duplicate it per episode. Never start from zero.

Problem 2: Writing timestamps manually by scrubbing the audio

Fix: Use your editor's chapter markers, or note rough timestamps during editing. Don't re-listen just to find them.

Problem 3: Rewriting the same promo blurb every week

Fix: Keep a "boilerplate" block for your show description, host bio, and standard links. Paste, don't rewrite.

Problem 4: Doing show notes, then separately doing social posts

Fix: Write them together. Your key takeaways are your social posts. Your quotes are your graphics. One pass, multiple outputs.

Problem 5: No consistency, so SEO never compounds

Fix: The same structure every episode means search engines (and listeners) know what to expect. Consistency beats cleverness.

---

The Repurposing Layer: Turn One Episode Into a Week of Content

This is where a template stops being a time-saver and starts being a growth engine. From the same notes you just wrote, you can pull:

    • 3–5 social posts — one per key takeaway, each with a hook line
    • 1 newsletter section — summary + top takeaway + link
    • 1 quote graphic — pull a strong line from the transcript
    • 1 YouTube description — reuse the summary, timestamps, and links
    • 1 short-form video script — the 30-second version of your best takeaway

The catch: doing this manually still takes time. The key is having prompts and formats ready so you're not thinking, just filling in.

---

A Quick Checklist Before You Publish

    • [ ] Title includes the core topic, not just an episode number
    • [ ] Summary answers "who is this for and what do they get"
    • [ ] 3–6 specific, non-vague key takeaways
    • [ ] Timestamps are accurate and labeled
    • [ ] All mentioned links are included and tested
    • [ ] One clear CTA
    • [ ] At least 3 social posts drafted from the takeaways
    • [ ] Transcript saved (even if not published)

Run this every episode and your show notes go from chore to asset.

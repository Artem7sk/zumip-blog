How to Verify If an AliExpress Supplier Is Legit for Dropshipping

You found a product with great margins. You're ready to launch. But there's one question standing between you and a profitable store: is this AliExpress supplier actually legit?

Trust me — this is the question that makes or breaks beginner dropshippers. A bad supplier means delayed shipments, defective products, disputes you can't win, and a store full of angry customers. A good supplier means repeat buyers and a business you can scale.

This guide walks you through a practical, step-by-step process to verify AliExpress suppliers before you commit. No fluff — just the exact checks I run on every supplier.

---

Why Supplier Verification Matters More Than Product Selection

Most dropshipping courses obsess over finding "winning products." But here's the uncomfortable truth: the same product from a bad supplier loses money, while it profits from a good one.

What actually goes wrong with unverified suppliers:

    • Shipping times that say "15–30 days" but take 45+
    • Wrong colors, sizes, or completely different items shipped
    • Fake tracking numbers that never update
    • Zero response when a customer wants a refund
    • Sudden price hikes after you've built your store around them

Verifying a supplier takes 15–20 minutes. Skipping that step can cost you weeks and hundreds of dollars in refunds and ad spend.

---

Step 1: Check the Store's Age and Overall Metrics

Open the supplier's store page on AliExpress and look at the header. You want to see:

    • Store age: At least 1–2 years. Newer stores aren't automatically bad, but they carry more risk.
    • Positive feedback percentage: Aim for 95%+. Below 90% is a red flag.
    • Total orders: Look for a store with a consistent track record, not just one viral product.
    • Followers: A healthy number relative to the store's age signals repeat buyers.

Red flags:

    • Store opened 3 months ago with 10,000 orders (possible, but verify hard)
    • Feedback percentage below 90%
    • No followers, no reviews, generic store name

---

Step 2: Analyze the Product Listing Itself

Click into the exact product you want to sell. Now dig deeper than the main image.

Check the reviews — but properly

Don't just read the 5-star reviews. Filter by:

    • 1–3 star reviews first. These reveal real problems: sizing, quality, shipping.
    • Reviews with photos. Customer photos show what actually arrives.
    • Reviews from your target country. A supplier with great US reviews may be terrible for EU shipping.

Check order volume and recency

    • Look for at least 50–100 orders on the specific listing.
    • Check that recent orders exist (last 30 days). A listing with 500 orders but nothing in 3 months is dead.

Check variants

If the listing has 20 color options but only 2 have reviews, the others may be dropship-only variants with unknown quality. Stick to variants with proven reviews.

---

Step 3: Message the Supplier (The Real Test)

This is where most dropshippers skip ahead — and it's the most important step. Send a message with specific questions:

1. "Do you offer ePacket or AliExpress Standard Shipping to [your country]? What's the average delivery time?"

2. "Can you ship without invoices or promotional materials in the package?"

3. "What's your processing time for orders placed on weekends?"

4. "Do you support custom packaging or branded inserts?" (If you plan to scale.)

5. "What happens if a customer receives a defective item?"

What a legit supplier does:

    • Replies within 24 hours (ideally 12)
    • Answers each question directly
    • Uses clear English
    • Offers solutions, not excuses

What a bad supplier does:

    • Replies with copy-paste "Dear friend, thanks for your message"
    • Ignores specific questions
    • Takes 3+ days to respond
    • Pushes you to buy first, ask later

Save these conversations. If you scale, you'll want a record of promises made.

---

Step 4: Place a Test Order (Yes, Actually)

Never launch a product without ordering it yourself first. Yes, it costs money and time. It's also the only way to know for sure.

When your test order arrives, check:

    • Packaging: Is it protective enough? Does it look professional?
    • Product quality: Does it match the listing photos?
    • Functionality: Does it actually work?
    • Shipping time: Did it arrive within the stated window?
    • Any branding/invoices: Did they include anything that reveals you're dropshipping?

If you can't order to your own country, order to a friend or use a forwarding service. This step alone will save you from 80% of supplier disasters.

---

Step 5: Score the Supplier Objectively

After running steps 1–4, you'll have a gut feeling. But gut feelings don't scale. Use a scorecard so you can compare multiple suppliers side by side.

A simple scoring framework:

| Criteria | Weight | Score (1–5) |

|---|---|---|

| Store age & feedback | 15% | |

| Product reviews quality | 20% | |

Suppliers scoring below 3.5 overall? Skip them. Above 4.0? Shortlist them and negotiate.

Step 6: Watch for These Advanced Red Flags

Once you've passed the basics, look for these:

    • Price changes without notice. Check the listing weekly. Sudden +20% jumps mean the supplier isn't stable.
    • Stock inconsistencies. If a variant keeps going out of stock, they're dropshipping from another supplier themselves.
    • Copy-pasted product descriptions. Indicates a reseller, not a manufacturer or authorized distributor.
    • No response to a follow-up message. If they ghost you after the first sale, that's your sign.
    • Suspiciously low prices. Below market rate often means counterfeit or bait-and-switch.

---

Step 7: Have a Backup Supplier Ready

Even legit suppliers fail. Holidays, factory issues, shipping delays — things happen. Before you launch, identify at least one backup supplier for every product.

Run the same verification process on the backup. Yes, it takes time. No, you won't regret it when your primary supplier goes dark during Q4.

---

Quick Verification Checklist

Before you commit to any AliExpress supplier, confirm:

    • [ ] Store is 1+ years old with 95%+ positive feedback
    • [ ] Product listing has 50+ orders and recent reviews
    • [ ] You read the 1–3 star reviews and customer photos
    • [ ] Supplier replied within 24 hours with specific answers
    • [ ] You placed and received a test order
    • [ ] Product quality matched the listing
    • [ ] Shipping time met your store's promise
    • [ ] Supplier scored 4.0+ on your scorecard
    • [ ] You have a verified backup supplier

If you can't tick every box, don't launch. Move to the next supplier.

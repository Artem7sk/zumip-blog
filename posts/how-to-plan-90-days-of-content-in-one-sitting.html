How to Plan 90 Days of Content in One Sitting

Most creators don't have a content problem. They have a planning problem.

You sit down to write, open a blank doc, and 45 minutes later you're still "thinking." Sound familiar? The fix isn't more motivation — it's a single, structured planning session that produces 90 days of content before you ever hit publish.

Here's the exact process for how to plan 90 days of content in one sitting, broken into blocks you can run in 3–4 hours.

---

Why One Sitting Beats Daily Planning

Daily planning has a hidden cost: context switching. Every time you decide "what should I post today?", you burn decision energy you could spend creating.

Planning 90 days at once flips the math:

    • One decision, 90 outputs. You choose themes once instead of 90 times.
    • Repurposing becomes visible. When you see everything side by side, cross-platform reuse is obvious.
    • Burnout drops. You stop waking up unsure what to make.

The goal isn't a perfect calendar. It's a draft you can execute against.

---

Before You Start: The 30-Minute Setup

Don't skip this. A one-sitting plan only works if the inputs are ready.

Gather these 4 things:

1. Your 3–5 core topics. The pillars you want to be known for. Example: "email marketing," "solo business ops," "content systems."

2. Your platforms. Pick max 3. Blog, YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn, newsletter — whatever you actually use.

3. Your formats. What you can realistically produce: short posts, long-form, video, carousel, email.

4. Your constraints. How many pieces per week? Be honest. 3 posts/week is better than a fantasy 7.

Write these on one page. This is your planning canvas.

---

Step 1: Brain-Dump 90 Ideas (60–90 Minutes)

This is the heavy lift. You're generating raw material, not polishing.

The rule: No editing, no judging, no "that's not good enough." Just volume.

Use a prompt structure to avoid blank-page paralysis. For each core topic, run through these angles:

    • Mistakes: "3 mistakes beginners make with [topic]"
    • Myths: "The biggest lie about [topic]"
    • Before/After: "What my [topic] looked like before vs. after"
    • Tools: "The 5 tools I use for [topic]"
    • Stories: "The time [topic] completely failed me"
    • Contrarian takes: "Why [common advice] is wrong"
    • Checklists: "The 7-point checklist for [topic]"
    • Q&A: "Answering the top 5 questions about [topic]"

If you have 5 topics × 8 angles × 3 variations, you have 120 ideas. You only need 90.

Pro tip: Keep a running "idea bank" file open all year. Every time a thought hits, drop it in. By your next planning session, you'll start with 20–30 ideas already loaded.

---

Step 2: Map Ideas to a 90-Day Calendar (45–60 Minutes)

Now you sort. Open a simple spreadsheet or calendar with 13 weeks.

Assign each week a theme. Example:

| Week | Theme |

|------|-------|

| 1 | Foundations |

| 2 | Common mistakes |

| 3 | Tools & workflows |

| 4 | Case study |

| ... | ... |

Themes create narrative momentum. Followers feel like they're on a journey, not getting random posts.

Then slot ideas into days. Don't overthink — just place them. You can shuffle later.

Match format to energy:

    • High-energy days → video or long-form
    • Low-energy days → short posts, quotes, reposts

---

Step 3: Build the Repurposing Matrix (30 Minutes)

This is where 90 days of content becomes 200+ pieces.

The matrix logic: One core idea → many formats.

Take a single blog post: "5 Email Subject Lines That Get Opened"

    • YouTube: 5-minute walkthrough
    • Instagram carousel: 5 slides, one line each
    • LinkedIn post: The #1 line + a story
    • Newsletter: Full breakdown + personal take
    • X/Twitter thread: One tweet per line
    • Short video: Hook + 3 best lines

That's 6 pieces from 1 idea. Multiply across 90 days and you're not "creating more" — you're redistributing what you already made.

Rule of thumb: For every long-form piece, plan 3–5 derivative posts before you move on.

---

Step 4: Stress-Test the Plan (20 Minutes)

Before you close the laptop, run this quick checklist:

    • [ ] Every week has a theme
    • [ ] No topic repeats within 30 days
    • [ ] Each long-form piece has 3+ repurposing slots
    • [ ] Weekly output matches your real capacity
    • [ ] At least 10 "easy win" posts are queued (quotes, tips, reposts)
    • [ ] One buffer week exists for when life happens

If any box is unchecked, fix it now — not in week 6 when you're behind.

---

The Mistake That Kills Most Plans

Creators plan 90 days, then treat the calendar as law. It's not.

Life shifts. Trends change. A post flops. The plan is a menu, not a contract.

Keep two things flexible:

1. The order — swap days freely.

2. The format — turn a planned video into a carousel if time is short.

What stays fixed: the themes and the repurposing links. Those are your system.

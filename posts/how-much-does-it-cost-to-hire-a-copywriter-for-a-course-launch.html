How Much Does It Cost to Hire a Copywriter for a Course Launch?

You've finished recording your course. The modules are uploaded, the bonuses are ready, the payment system is connected. There's just one problem: none of it sells until someone writes the copy that makes people want to buy it.

So you start looking for a copywriter — and the quotes you get back range from $150 to $15,000. That gap isn't random. It reflects real differences in scope, skill, and risk. This article breaks down what actually drives the price, so you can decide whether to hire, DIY, or split the difference.

The Short Answer: Real Price Ranges

Copywriting for a course launch isn't one deliverable — it's a bundle. Here's what the market roughly looks like:

    • Budget / new freelancers: $150–$500 for a single sales page
    • Mid-tier specialists: $800–$3,000 for a sales page, or $1,500–$5,000 for a full launch package
    • Experienced conversion copywriters: $3,000–$10,000+ for a complete launch (sales page, email sequence, ads, social)
    • Agencies and launch teams: $10,000–$25,000+, often bundled with strategy and funnel build-out

The same words can cost $200 or $8,000. What separates them isn't the number of words — it's what's included.

What Actually Drives the Price

1. Scope of deliverables

A single sales page is one project. A launch is a system. Ask any copywriter what's included before you compare quotes, because "launch copy" means different things to different people.

A typical full launch package includes:

    • Sales page (long-form, often 3,000–6,000 words)
    • 5–10 launch emails (cart open, value, objection-handling, cart close)
    • 3–5 social posts or ad variations
    • Thank-you and onboarding emails
    • Sometimes: webinar script, waitlist sequence, upsell page

If one quote covers all of that and another covers only the sales page, they aren't comparable — even if the numbers look similar.

2. Research and strategy time

Good copywriters don't start with the blank page. They start with your audience. That means:

    • Interviewing you about your students' pain points
    • Reading reviews, testimonials, and support tickets
    • Studying competitors' offers
    • Mapping objections and building a message hierarchy

This research phase is often 30–50% of the project time. It's also the part beginners skip — and it's why their copy reads generic.

3. Conversion experience

A copywriter who has written 50 launch emails knows which subject lines get opened and which promises fall flat. That pattern recognition is what you're paying for at the higher tiers. It's not about prettier sentences — it's about knowing where buyers hesitate and what language moves them past it.

4. Deadlines and revisions

Rush timelines cost more. Unlimited revisions cost more (or get priced in). Ask upfront:

    • How many revision rounds are included?
    • What's the turnaround time?
    • What happens if the copy underperforms?

What You're Really Paying For (And When It's Worth It)

Hiring a copywriter makes financial sense when:

    • Your course is priced above $500. A $2,000 copywriter on a $997 course needs just 2–3 extra sales to break even.
    • You have an audience but low conversion. If 1,000 people visit your sales page and 5 buy, copy is likely the bottleneck.
    • You're launching repeatedly. A reusable framework pays for itself across multiple launches.
    • Your time is worth more than the fee. If writing the launch takes you 40 hours, that's 40 hours not spent on delivery or support.

Hiring makes less sense when your course is under $200, you're pre-revenue, or you genuinely enjoy writing and have the time to iterate.

The DIY Route: Cheaper, But Not Free

If the quotes came back higher than expected, DIY is a legitimate path — with one caveat: your first draft will probably underperform. Budget for 2–3 revision cycles based on real reader feedback.

To make DIY work, you need three things:

1. A proven structure. Don't invent a sales page from scratch. Use frameworks that already convert.

2. Swipe files. Study copy that sells courses similar to yours. Note the sequence of arguments, not just the wording.

3. A feedback loop. Get 5–10 people from your target audience to read the page and tell you where they got confused or skeptical.

The trade-off is time. Expect 15–30 hours for a full launch package if you're doing it properly.

How to Vet a Copywriter Before You Pay

If you do decide to hire, don't skip this step. Ask for:

    • Two relevant samples. Not just any copy — course or info-product launches specifically.
    • A short test paid assignment. $100–$300 for one email or a sales page section. Cheap insurance.
    • Their research process. If they don't ask about your audience, walk away.
    • References from past launches. Ask what the conversion rate was before and after.

Red flags: guarantees of specific sales numbers, no questions about your offer, and quotes that are suspiciously low for the scope.

A Simple Decision Framework

| Your situation | Best move |

|---|---|

| Course under $200, first launch | DIY with templates |

| Course $500–$2,000, some audience | Templates + light freelance help |

| Course $2,000+, established audience | Hire a mid-tier specialist |

| Six-figure launch target | Full launch team or senior copywriter |

The Bottom Line

So how much does it cost to hire a copywriter for a course launch? Anywhere from a few hundred dollars to five figures — and the number that matters is the one that fits your course price and launch stage. A $5,000 copywriter on a $97 course is a losing bet. A $49 template pack on a $997 course with 200 warm leads can pay for itself with a single sale.

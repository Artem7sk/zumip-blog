How to Rewrite a Course Sales Page That Is Not Converting

You finished the course. You built the modules, recorded the lessons, maybe even got a few beta students through. Then you wrote a sales page, hit publish, and… nothing. Or worse: traffic comes in, people scroll, and they leave without buying.

If your course sales page isn't converting, the problem is almost never the course itself. It's almost always the copy. The good news is that a sales page is one of the easiest assets to fix, because you can rewrite it in an afternoon and test it the same week.

This guide walks you through a practical, step-by-step process to rewrite a course sales page that is not converting — with specific checks, examples, and a checklist you can apply today.

---

Step 1: Diagnose Why It's Not Converting Before You Rewrite

Most creators jump straight to rewriting headlines. That's a mistake. You need to know what's broken first, or you'll rewrite the same mistakes in new words.

Run through this quick diagnostic:

    • Low traffic + low conversion? You may have a traffic problem, not a copy problem. Don't rewrite yet.
    • Decent traffic + people bounce in the first 10 seconds? Your headline and opening are failing.
    • People scroll to the pricing section but don't buy? Your offer, proof, or risk-reversal is weak.
    • People click "Enroll" but abandon checkout? That's a checkout/UX issue, not copy.
    • People buy but refund? Your promise doesn't match the course.

Write down which of these matches your situation. That's your rewrite focus.

---

Step 2: Audit the Five Sections That Kill Conversions

Almost every underperforming course sales page fails in one of five places. Check each one honestly.

1. The Headline

Your headline has one job: make the right person feel seen in under five seconds.

Weak: "Learn Digital Marketing in 8 Weeks"

Stronger: "Go From Zero to Your First Paying Client in 8 Weeks — Even If You've Never Run an Ad"

The second one names the audience, the outcome, and removes an objection.

2. The Opening (First 100 Words)

Most sales pages start with the creator's story. Don't. Start with the reader's problem.

Bad opening:

> "Hi, I'm Sarah. I've been in marketing for 12 years and I love helping people…"

Better opening:

> "You've watched 40 hours of free YouTube tutorials. You still don't have a client. Here's why — and what actually works."

3. The Promise / Transformation

State the before-and-after clearly. Vague promises don't sell.

    • ❌ "Master social media marketing"
    • ✅ "Post 3 times a week and book 2 discovery calls per week within 30 days"

4. The Proof

No testimonials? That's a conversion killer. If you have zero students yet, use:

    • Beta tester feedback (even casual DMs)
    • Your own results
    • Screenshots of the process, not just outcomes
    • A "founding student" offer to get your first testimonials

5. The Offer & Risk Reversal

People hesitate at the price. Your job is to make the decision feel safe.

    • Add a clear refund policy
    • Show what's included as a tangible list
    • Add a "who this is NOT for" section — it builds trust

---

Step 3: Rewrite With a Proven Framework (Not Vibes)

Freewriting a sales page rarely works. Use a structure. A simple one that consistently converts:

1. Hook — name the pain

2. Agitate — show what happens if nothing changes

3. Solution — introduce your course as the bridge

4. Credibility — why you, why now

5. What's inside — module-by-module, benefit-led

6. Proof — testimonials, results, screenshots

7. Offer stack — everything they get, with value

8. Price + guarantee — remove risk

9. FAQ — handle the last objections

10. Final CTA — one clear action

Step 4: Rewrite the Headline (Do This 5 Times)

Write five versions of your headline using different angles:

1. Outcome-led: "Land your first freelance client in 30 days"

2. Problem-led: "Stop posting content nobody buys from"

3. Audience-led: "For coaches who hate selling on calls"

4. Contrarian: "Why posting daily is killing your course sales"

5. Time-bound: "From idea to paid course in one weekend"

Pick the one that feels most specific. Specific always beats clever.

---

Step 5: Rewrite the CTA Buttons

"Buy Now" is lazy. Match the CTA to the reader's emotional state:

    • "Enroll Now" → neutral
    • "Start My First Module Today" → action-oriented
    • "Get Instant Access" → speed-focused
    • "Join the Next Cohort" → urgency/community

Test two versions. Even small changes here can move conversion.

---

Step 6: Add the Missing Trust Elements

If your page lacks these, add them before you do anything else:

    • [ ] Real testimonials with names and photos (or initials if private)
    • [ ] A short "About the instructor" section with credentials
    • [ ] Refund policy stated plainly
    • [ ] FAQ answering: "How long do I have access?", "Is this for beginners?", "What if it's not for me?"
    • [ ] A visible price (hiding it kills trust)

---

Step 7: Fix the Mobile Experience

Over half your traffic is mobile. Check:

    • Does the headline fit above the fold on a phone?
    • Are CTA buttons thumb-reachable?
    • Is the page loading in under 3 seconds?
    • Are videos auto-playing with sound? (Turn that off.)

---

Step 8: Test, Don't Guess

Once you've rewritten the page, test one variable at a time:

1. Headline (biggest lever)

2. Opening paragraph

3. CTA button text

4. Price framing (e.g., "3 payments of $X" vs. "$X")

Use a simple tool or even just track click-through on two versions for a week. Don't change five things at once — you won't know what worked.

---

Quick Checklist: Rewrite Your Course Sales Page This Week

    • [ ] Diagnosed the real conversion problem
    • [ ] Audited headline, opening, promise, proof, offer
    • [ ] Rewrote using a proven framework
    • [ ] Wrote 5 headline variations and picked the most specific
    • [ ] Replaced generic CTAs with action-based ones
    • [ ] Added testimonials, FAQ, and refund policy
    • [ ] Verified mobile experience
    • [ ] Set up a simple A/B test

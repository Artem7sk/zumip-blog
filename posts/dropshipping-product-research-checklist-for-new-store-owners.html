Dropshipping Product Research Checklist for New Store Owners

Most new dropshippers don't fail because they picked a "bad" product. They fail because they picked a product without checking anything. They see a viral TikTok, get excited, import it to their store, and only discover the 8% margin or the 40-day shipping time after the first five orders come in.

This guide is a practical, step-by-step dropshipping product research checklist you can run for every product before you spend a dollar on ads. Work through it in order — each section filters out a category of failure.

---

Step 1: Validate Demand Before Anything Else

A product can look amazing and still have zero buyers. Before you analyze margins or suppliers, confirm people actually search for and buy this thing.

Check these signals:

    • Search volume: Use Google Keyword Planner or a free tool like Keyword Surfer. Look for at least a few hundred monthly searches for your main keyword in your target country. Niche products can work with less, but near-zero search volume usually means near-zero buyers.
    • Google Trends: Check a 12-month and 5-year view. You want a stable or growing line — not a spike that peaked three months ago. Seasonal spikes are fine if you plan around them.
    • Marketplace proof: Search the product on Amazon and Etsy. Are there listings with hundreds of reviews? That confirms demand. Are the reviews recent? That confirms current demand.
    • Ad library check: Open Meta Ad Library and search your product. If you see the same advertiser running the same ad for 2+ months, that's a strong signal it's profitable. Nobody keeps paying for losing ads.

Red flag: You can't find demand anywhere except one viral video. Viral ≠ profitable.

---

Step 2: The Margin Math (Do This Before You Fall in Love)

New store owners consistently underestimate costs. Here's the formula that keeps you honest:

Landed cost = product cost + shipping to customer + payment processing + platform fees

Then:

Contribution margin = Selling price − landed cost − ad cost per acquisition

Run it with real numbers. Example:

| Item | Amount |

|---|---|

| Selling price | $39.99 |

| Product + shipping from supplier | $12.00 |

| Payment processing (~3%) | $1.20 |

| App / platform fees | $0.50 |

| Gross profit before ads | $26.29 |

| Target CPA (cost per acquisition) | $15.00 |

| Contribution margin | $11.29 |

Rules of thumb that hold up in practice:

    • Aim for a minimum 3x markup on product cost (sell for at least 3x what you pay).
    • Your gross margin should be 60%+ before ad spend. Below that, paid traffic usually eats you alive.
    • If the product only works at a $60+ price point, ask whether a beginner store can realistically convert cold traffic at that price.

If the math is thin, no amount of good creative will save it.

---

Step 3: Product Criteria Checklist

Not every profitable-math product is a good dropshipping product. Run these filters:

    • [ ] Lightweight — under ~2 lbs / 1 kg where possible. Shipping cost and speed both improve.
    • [ ] Not fragile — glass, ceramics, and liquids cause refund headaches.
    • [ ] Not electronics with batteries — shipping restrictions, defect rates, and support load.
    • [ ] Hard to find in local stores — if Walmart carries it for $9.99, you can't win.
    • [ ] Solves a visible problem or has "wow" factor — boring commodities don't stop the scroll.
    • [ ] No trademarked characters or logos — instant takedown risk.
    • [ ] Not a health claim product — "cures anxiety" gets your ad account banned.
    • [ ] Has room for bundles/upsells — accessories, refills, or multi-packs raise average order value.
    • [ ] Ships in under 15 days to your main market — customers tolerate 7–12 days. Beyond 20, refunds spike.

Red flag: You have to explain why the product is good. If it needs a paragraph of justification, it won't sell from an ad.

---

Step 4: Supplier Vetting (Where Most Beginners Get Burned)

A great product with a bad supplier is a failed store. Before you list anything, vet at least 3 suppliers for the same product.

What to check:

1. Platform vs. direct: AliExpress and CJdropshipping are fine to start, but ask about their processing time (not just shipping time — these are different).

2. Processing time: How many days before the item leaves the warehouse? Anything over 3–5 days is a problem.

3. Shipping method and tracking: Insist on tracked shipping with a real tracking number. Untracked = "where is my order" emails = chargebacks.

4. Communication test: Send a pre-sale question. How fast and how clearly do they answer? This predicts how they'll handle a defect.

5. Defect and refund policy: Ask in writing. "What happens if the item arrives broken?"

6. Sample order: Order one unit to yourself. Yes, it costs money and time. It's the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.

7. US/EU warehouse option: If available, this cuts shipping from 15+ days to 3–7. Worth a slightly higher unit cost.

Score each supplier on price, processing time, shipping time, communication, and policy. Pick the best total, not the cheapest unit price.

---

Step 5: Competition Reality Check

You don't need zero competition — you need a reason to win.

    • How many stores sell this exact product? If it's 50+ identical stores with the same AliExpress photos, skip it unless you can differentiate.
    • What's their angle? Price, bundle, branding, speed, or niche audience. Pick one you can beat.
    • Can you improve the offer? Better photos, a bundle, a guarantee, faster shipping, or a niche-specific version.
    • Check their reviews: What do customers complain about? That complaint is your positioning.

Red flag: The only differentiator you can think of is "cheaper." You'll lose that war.

---

Step 6: Test Small Before You Scale

Once a product passes all five steps above, test it with a small budget:

1. One product, one audience, one creative angle.

2. Small daily budget for 3–5 days.

3. Watch add-to-cart rate and cost per add-to-cart first — they tell you if the product resonates before you judge full conversions.

4. Kill fast if CTR and ATC are weak. Scale only what shows early traction.

Don't "fix" a product that failed the checklist. Move to the next one.

---

The Full Checklist (Copy This)

Demand

    • [ ] Search volume confirmed
    • [ ] Google Trends stable/growing
    • [ ] Amazon/Etsy reviews recent
    • [ ] Meta Ad Library shows long-running ads

Margin

    • [ ] 3x+ markup
    • [ ] 60%+ gross margin before ads
    • [ ] Landed cost calculated with real numbers

Product

    • [ ] Lightweight, not fragile, no batteries
    • [ ] Not in local stores cheaply
    • [ ] No trademark/health claims
    • [ ] Bundle/upsell potential
    • [ ] Ships under 15 days

Supplier

    • [ ] 3+ suppliers compared
    • [ ] Processing time under 5 days
    • [ ] Tracked shipping confirmed
    • [ ] Communication tested
    • [ ] Sample ordered

Competition

    • [ ] Clear differentiation angle
    • [ ] Offer can be improved

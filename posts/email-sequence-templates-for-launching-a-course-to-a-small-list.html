Email Sequence Templates for Launching a Course to a Small List

You don't need 10,000 subscribers to launch a course successfully. A small, engaged list of 200–500 people can generate real revenue—if your email sequence is built around relationship, clarity, and a genuine offer.

The problem? Most course creators freeze at the writing stage. They stare at a blank email draft, unsure what to say, when to say it, or how to ask for the sale without feeling pushy.

This guide gives you a practical, fill-in-the-blank framework for email sequence templates you can adapt for a small list launch. No fluff, no "just be authentic" advice—just structure you can use today.

---

Why Small Lists Need a Different Launch Approach

Big launches rely on volume. Small lists rely on trust.

With a list of 300 people, you can't afford to send generic, corporate-sounding emails. You also can't afford to "warm up" for three weeks and hope people remember you. Every email needs to do a job.

A small-list launch sequence should:

    • Feel personal. Write like you're emailing one person, not a crowd.
    • Be specific. Vague promises ("transform your life!") don't convert. Concrete outcomes do.
    • Have a clear timeline. Open cart, close cart. Deadlines create decisions.
    • Include real proof. Testimonials, screenshots, student results—even from beta testers.

Now let's build the sequence.

---

The Core Email Sequence Templates (7 Emails, 10 Days)

This is a proven structure for a small-list course launch. Adjust the timing to fit your schedule, but keep the order.

Email 1: The "Something's Coming" Email (Day 1)

Goal: Plant a seed. No selling yet.

Subject line ideas:

    • "I've been working on something…"
    • "Quick question for you"

Body structure:

1. Open with a relatable problem your audience has.

2. Hint that you've been building a solution.

3. Ask a soft question to spark replies (replies boost deliverability).

Example snippet:

> "Quick question—what's the #1 thing stopping you from [desired outcome]? I'm putting the final touches on something that might help, and I want to make sure it actually solves the right problem."

---

Email 2: The Story + Lesson Email (Day 3)

Goal: Build connection and authority.

Body structure:

1. Tell a short story about your own struggle with the problem.

2. Share the turning point or lesson learned.

3. Transition: "That's exactly why I created [Course Name]."

Tip: Keep it under 300 words. Small lists reward intimacy, not essays.

---

Email 3: The Value-First Email (Day 4)

Goal: Give something useful before you ask for anything.

Body structure:

1. Share one actionable tip related to your course topic.

2. Explain why it works.

3. Mention that the full system is inside your course.

Example:

> "Here's a 5-minute exercise you can do today: [specific tip]. If you want the complete framework, I'll be opening enrollment for [Course Name] tomorrow."

---

Email 4: The Cart Open Email (Day 5)

Goal: Announce enrollment is live.

Body structure:

1. Clear subject line: "[Course Name] is now open"

2. One-sentence summary of what the course helps people do.

3. Bullet list of 3–5 key outcomes.

4. Direct link to the sales page.

5. Mention the cart close date.

Don't over-explain here. The sales page does the heavy lifting.

---

Email 5: The Objection-Handling Email (Day 6)

Goal: Address the top 3 reasons people hesitate.

Body structure:

1. "I got a few questions about [Course Name], so let me answer them here."

2. Address objections like:

    • "I don't have time."
    • "Will this work for my situation?"
    • "Is it worth the price?"
3. Reframe each objection with a specific answer.

---

Email 6: The Social Proof Email (Day 8)

Goal: Let others sell for you.

Body structure:

1. Share 2–3 testimonials or results from beta students.

2. Add a short line about what made the difference for them.

3. Remind readers the cart closes in 48 hours.

If you have no testimonials yet: Use beta feedback, screenshots of DMs, or your own before/after story.

---

Email 7: The "Last Call" Email (Day 10)

Goal: Create urgency without manipulation.

Body structure:

1. Subject line: "Closing tonight" or "Last chance"

2. Short, direct message: cart closes at [time].

3. Recap: who the course is for, what they get, price.

4. One final link.

Keep it short. Long last-call emails get skimmed anyway.

---

A Quick Checklist Before You Hit Send

    • [ ] Every email has one clear CTA (not three).
    • [ ] Subject lines are under 50 characters where possible.
    • [ ] You've linked to the sales page in every email from Day 5 onward.
    • [ ] You've mentioned the cart close date at least three times.
    • [ ] You've personalized where it counts (first name, their specific problem).
    • [ ] You've read each email out loud to check the tone.

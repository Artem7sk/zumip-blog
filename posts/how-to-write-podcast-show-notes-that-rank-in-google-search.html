How to Write Podcast Show Notes That Rank in Google Search

Most podcasters treat show notes as an afterthought — a quick paragraph, a list of links, done. Then they wonder why their episodes never show up in Google. The truth is simple: Google can't listen to your audio. It reads text. If your show notes are thin, unstructured, or missing the words people actually search for, your episode is invisible to search engines.

This guide walks you through a repeatable system for writing show notes that serve two audiences at once: the human deciding whether to hit play, and the crawler deciding whether to index your page. Let's break it down step by step.

---

Why Most Show Notes Fail at SEO

Before fixing the problem, understand what's broken. Common mistakes:

    • Two-line notes. "In this episode, we talk about marketing. Links below." Google has nothing to rank.
    • No episode-specific keywords. The notes describe the show, not the topic people search for.
    • Wall-of-text formatting. No headings, no lists, no structure — hard for readers and crawlers alike.
    • Duplicate boilerplate. The same intro paragraph on all 200 episodes dilutes relevance.
    • Missing metadata. No timestamps, no guest names, no transcript. You're leaving free keyword real estate on the table.

The fix isn't more writing. It's smarter writing with a structure you can reuse every single episode.

---

Step 1: Pick One Primary Keyword Per Episode

Every episode should target one clear search intent. Ask: If someone wanted to find this conversation, what would they type into Google?

Examples:

    • Episode about email list growth → `"how to grow an email list without ads"`
    • Episode interviewing a sleep scientist → `"sleep scientist podcast"` or `"why you wake up at 3am"`

Use that phrase naturally in:

    • The episode title
    • The first 100 words of your show notes
    • At least one H2 subheading

Don't force it. If it doesn't fit, pick a different phrase.

---

Step 2: Structure Your Show Notes Like a Blog Post

Here's a template you can copy for every episode:

1. Episode Title (H1)

Include the guest name and the core topic. Example:

`Ep. 47: How to Grow an Email List Without Paid Ads — with Jane Doe`

2. One-Paragraph Summary

2–4 sentences. Lead with the keyword. Tell the reader what they'll learn and why it matters.

3. Key Takeaways (Bulleted List)

5–7 bullets. Each one should be a specific insight, not a vague teaser.

❌ "Jane shares her thoughts on email marketing."

✅ "Why a 3-email welcome sequence outperforms a 7-email one for solo creators."

4. Timestamps / Chapter Markers

This is huge for SEO and user experience. Format them clearly:

```

[00:00] Intro

[03:12] Why most email lists die in month two

[11:45] The 3-email sequence that works

[22:30] Tools Jane actually uses

[34:10] Common mistakes to avoid

```

Google often pulls these into featured snippets for "podcast timestamp" queries.

5. Guest Bio + Links

Short bio (2–3 sentences) with the guest's name spelled correctly. Link to their site, book, or socials. This drives backlink opportunities when guests share the episode.

6. Resources Mentioned

List every book, tool, article, or person mentioned. This is where long-tail keywords naturally appear.

7. Full Transcript (Optional but Powerful)

Transcripts can triple your word count and index hundreds of extra keyword variations. If you don't have one, at least include a "highlights" section with 3–5 quoted lines.

---

Step 3: Optimize the Page Elements Around the Text

Show notes don't rank in isolation. The page they live on matters.

    • Meta description: 150–160 characters. Include the keyword and a reason to click.
    • URL slug: `yourdomain.com/podcast/grow-email-list-without-ads` — not `/episode-47`.
    • Image alt text: Describe the episode cover or guest photo with the keyword where natural.
    • Internal links: Link to 2–3 related episodes. This builds topical authority across your podcast archive.
    • Schema markup: Use `PodcastEpisode` schema if your host supports it. It helps Google display episode info in search results.

---

Step 4: Write for Humans First, Crawlers Second

Google's helpful content systems reward pages that genuinely answer questions. So:

    • Use plain language. No jargon unless your audience uses it.
    • Answer the question in the notes, not just in the audio. If the episode is about "how to price a course," write a 100-word answer in the notes.
    • Front-load value. The first 150 words should tell the reader exactly what they'll get.

A useful test: If someone read only your show notes and never listened, would they learn something? If yes, you're on the right track.

Quick SEO Checklist for Every Episode

Before you hit publish, run through this:

    • [ ] Primary keyword chosen and used in title + first paragraph + one H2
    • [ ] Summary is 2–4 sentences and answers a real question
    • [ ] At least 5 specific bullet takeaways
    • [ ] Timestamps formatted with `[MM:SS]` markers
    • [ ] Guest bio with correct name spelling and links
    • [ ] Resources section with all mentioned tools/books
    • [ ] Meta description written (150–160 chars)
    • [ ] URL slug is descriptive, not numeric
    • [ ] 2–3 internal links to related episodes
    • [ ] Image alt text filled in

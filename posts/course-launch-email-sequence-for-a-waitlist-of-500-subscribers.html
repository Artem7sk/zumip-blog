Course Launch Email Sequence for a Waitlist of 500 Subscribers: A Step-by-Step Playbook

You've built the course. You've collected 500 emails on a waitlist. Now comes the part most creators dread: turning that list into buyers without burning it out, annoying people, or sounding like a discount megaphone.

A waitlist of 500 subscribers is a specific asset. It's small enough that you can treat people like individuals, but large enough that a sloppy sequence will cost you real revenue. This guide walks through a concrete, repeatable email sequence you can adapt to your launch — with structure, timing, and copy angles for each email.

---

Why 500 Waitlist Subscribers Is a Real Opportunity

Before the tactics, get the mindset right. A 500-person waitlist is not "just a small list." It's a warm audience that already raised their hand and said "tell me when this is ready." That's a different relationship than a cold list.

What that means practically:

    • You can afford personal touches. Reply to early openers. Reference specific questions people asked.
    • Segmentation matters more than volume. Even basic engagement-based segments (openers vs. non-openers) will lift results.
    • You don't need hype — you need clarity. Your job is to remove doubt, not manufacture urgency you don't have.

A well-structured course launch email sequence for a waitlist of 500 subscribers typically runs 8–12 emails over 10–14 days, split into three phases: pre-launch, open cart, and close cart.

---

Phase 1: Pre-Launch (3–4 Emails Before Cart Opens)

The goal here isn't selling. It's priming. You're building the mental case for why this course exists and why now is the moment.

Email 1 — The "Something's Coming" Note

Timing: 5–7 days before cart opens.

Short. Personal. One idea: something is coming, and here's the specific problem it solves.

    • Subject line angle: "I finally finished the thing you asked about"
    • Body: 150–200 words max. Tell the story of why you built it. End with a soft line: "Details drop [day]. Keep an eye on your inbox."

Email 2 — The Problem Deep-Dive

Timing: 3–4 days before launch.

Pick the single biggest pain your course solves. Describe it so accurately that readers think you're reading their mind.

    • Use a mini-story or a real (anonymized) student scenario.
    • Don't mention the course yet — or mention it only as a teaser.

Email 3 — The Proof Email

Timing: 1–2 days before launch.

Show that the approach works. This could be:

    • A case study or before/after from a beta tester
    • A breakdown of your own result
    • A screenshot or testimonial (only if it's real)

Email 4 — The "Tomorrow" Reminder

Timing: Day before cart opens.

One job: make sure people know when to check their inbox. Include the exact time and what they'll get (bonus, early-bird price, etc., if applicable).

---

Phase 2: Open Cart (4–5 Emails During the Sale Window)

Now you're selling. But "selling" doesn't mean repeating "buy now." Each email should answer a different objection.

Email 5 — The Launch Email

Timing: Day 1 of cart open.

This is your workhorse. Structure it like this:

1. Hook — restate the problem in one line

2. Reveal — what the course is and who it's for

3. What's inside — 3–5 bullets, outcome-focused

4. Who it's NOT for — this builds trust and reduces refunds

5. Price + link — clear, no games

6. FAQ teaser — 2–3 quick answers

7. PS — restate the deadline

Email 6 — The "Is This For Me?" Email

Timing: Day 2–3.

List 5–6 specific situations. "This is for you if…" and "This isn't for you if…" This single email often outperforms the launch email because it does the qualifying work for the reader.

Email 7 — The Objection Crusher

Timing: Day 4–5.

Pick the top 3 objections you hear (price, time, "will this work for my niche"). Answer each in 2–3 sentences. Don't over-explain.

Email 8 — The Social Proof Stack

Timing: Day 6–7.

Stack testimonials, screenshots, or results. Add one line of context per piece of proof so it doesn't feel like a wall of quotes.

Email 9 — The FAQ / Behind-the-Scenes

Timing: Day 8–9.

Answer the questions people actually emailed you. If nobody emailed, answer the questions you know they're thinking.

---

Phase 3: Close Cart (2–3 Emails in the Final 48 Hours)

This is where most creators leave money on the table. The last 48 hours typically drive a disproportionate share of sales — but only if you show up.

Email 10 — The "48 Hours Left" Email

Timing: 2 days before close.

Straightforward. Deadline. Recap of what they get. Link.

Email 11 — The "Last Chance" Email

Timing: Morning of close day.

Short. Urgent without being manipulative. One paragraph, one link.

Email 12 — The Final Hours Email

Timing: 3–6 hours before close.

Two or three sentences. "Cart closes at [time]. Here's the link." That's it.

---

Practical Tips for a 500-Person Waitlist Specifically

    • Segment by opens. After email 5, split your list into openers and non-openers. Send a re-engagement subject line to non-openers.
    • Reply to real replies. At 500 people, you can actually answer responses. Do it — it converts.
    • Don't send more than one email per day. With a small list, fatigue is real and unsubscribes hurt more.
    • Track clicks, not just opens. Open rates are noisy post-Apple MPP. Clicks tell you who's actually interested.
    • Reuse winners. Whichever email gets the most clicks, send a variation of it again near close.

---

Quick Checklist Before You Hit Send

    • [ ] Every email has one clear job
    • [ ] Subject lines are tested against a swipe file, not improvised
    • [ ] Cart-open and cart-close dates are in every relevant email
    • [ ] There's a PS in the sales emails
    • [ ] You've written the "is this for me" email
    • [ ] Non-openers get a separate re-engagement sequence
    • [ ] You have a plan for the final 6 hours

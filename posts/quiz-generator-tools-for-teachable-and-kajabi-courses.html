Quiz Generator Tools for Teachable and Kajabi Courses: How to Create Assessments That Actually Boost Completion

If you run a course on Teachable or Kajabi, you already know the dirty secret of the online education world: most students don't finish. They buy, they binge the first module, and then life gets in the way. One of the most effective ways to fight this is assessment — quizzes, checkpoints, and reflection prompts that keep learners engaged and give them a reason to come back.

The problem? Building good quizzes takes time. And most course creators aren't instructional designers.

That's where quiz generator tools come in. In this article, we'll look at how quiz generators work with Teachable and Kajabi, what to look for, and a practical workflow you can use this week to turn your existing lessons into engaging assessments.

---

Why Quizzes Matter More Than You Think

Before we get into tools, let's be clear about why you should care.

    • Retention: Retrieval practice (actively recalling information) is one of the most well-supported findings in learning science. Students who quiz themselves remember more than students who just re-read.
    • Completion rates: Quizzes create micro-commitments. A student who finishes Module 2's quiz is far more likely to start Module 3.
    • Feedback loop: Quiz results tell you which lessons are confusing. That's free product research.
    • Upsell positioning: Students who pass a quiz feel competent — and competent students buy advanced courses.

If your course has zero assessments, you're leaving engagement (and revenue) on the table.

---

How Quiz Generators Integrate with Teachable and Kajabi

Both platforms have native quiz features, but they're limited. Here's the honest breakdown:

Teachable

    • Native quizzes: Available inside lectures as multiple-choice questions. Simple, but no branching, no case studies, no open-ended prompts.
    • Best use: Quick knowledge checks at the end of a video.
    • Workaround: Use external tools (Typeform, Google Forms, Interact) and embed via HTML block, or link out.

Kajabi

    • Native quizzes: Kajabi has a dedicated Quiz feature that's more robust than Teachable's — you can build multi-question assessments with scoring and pass/fail logic.
    • Best use: Module-level assessments, certification-style checkpoints.
    • Workaround: For richer formats (case studies, discussion prompts), pair Kajabi quizzes with downloadable worksheets or community posts.

The key insight: Native tools handle multiple choice. They don't handle engagement. That's where a quiz generator — or a template library — becomes essential.

What to Look for in a Quiz Generator Tool

Not all quiz tools are created equal. Here's a checklist:

1. Export flexibility — Can you export to CSV, GIFT, or QTI format? This matters if you want to bulk-upload into Kajabi or an LMS.

2. Question variety — Multiple choice is table stakes. Look for scenario-based questions, case studies, and open reflection prompts.

3. AI assistance — Can it generate questions from your existing lesson content? This is the single biggest time-saver.

4. Branding control — Your quiz should look like your course, not like a generic form.

5. Analytics — You want to see which questions students get wrong. That's how you improve your course.

6. Cost — Many tools charge $30–$99/month. For solo course creators, that adds up fast.

A Practical Workflow: From Lesson to Quiz in 20 Minutes

Here's a step-by-step process you can run today.

Step 1: Pick one lesson

Don't try to quiz your whole course at once. Start with your highest-value lesson — the one where students must understand the concept before moving on.

Step 2: Extract 3–5 key takeaways

Write them down as plain sentences. Example:

    • "A landing page needs one clear CTA."
    • "Social proof should appear above the fold."
    • "Headlines outperform images in A/B tests."

Step 3: Turn each takeaway into a question

    • Recall: "What's the minimum number of CTAs a landing page should have?"
    • Application: "Your client's page has three CTAs. What's the first thing you'd fix?"
    • Reflection: "Look at your own landing page. How many CTAs does it have?"

Step 4: Add one scenario question

Scenario questions are the secret weapon. They force students to think, not just remember. Example: "A student's conversion rate is 0.5%. Their headline is vague and they have no testimonials. Which fix should they prioritize first, and why?"

Step 5: Upload to Teachable or Kajabi

    • Teachable: Add questions directly to the lecture, or embed an external form.
    • Kajabi: Use the Quiz feature, or attach a downloadable worksheet in the lesson.

Step 6: Review analytics after 30 days

Which questions had the lowest pass rate? That lesson needs re-recording or a supplementary resource.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Quizzing trivia instead of concepts. Nobody cares what year a framework was invented. Quiz the application.

2. Making quizzes too hard. A quiz that everyone fails doesn't motivate — it demoralizes. Aim for 70–80% pass rates.

3. Skipping feedback. Every wrong answer should explain why it's wrong. This is where learning happens.

4. No connection to the next lesson. End each quiz with a teaser: "Next, we'll apply this to your actual funnel."

5. Ignoring mobile. Over half your students will take quizzes on their phones. Test it.

    • [ ] Pass threshold set between 70–80%
    • [ ] Tested on mobile
    • [ ] Analytics tracking enabled
    • [ ] Next lesson teased at the end
